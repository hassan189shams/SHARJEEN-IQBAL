01 / Commitments

A voice that listens.
A plan that follows through.

Representation should be more than a slogan. These are proposed priorities to discuss with students and pursue through appropriate university channels—not promises of outcomes beyond a student representative’s control.

01
Academic support

Clear information, fewer surprises

Raise recurring concerns about timetables, examination notices, results and academic communication with the relevant offices.

02
Department representation

Every department deserves a voice

Make it easier to share department-level concerns and track whether they have been referred and answered.

03
Hostel and hall life

Listen where students live

Bring forward concerns about dining, cleanliness, maintenance, water and shared facilities through appropriate channels.

04
Campus facilities

Document problems, not just complaints

Build a clear issue log for concerns about libraries, laboratories, classrooms, Wi-Fi and common spaces.

05
Student welfare

Respect, dignity and fair treatment

Listen carefully and help students find the appropriate official support or grievance channel when needed.

06
Career readiness

Make opportunities easier to find

Advocate for clearer access to information about internships, career events, skill development and placement resources.

07
Transparency

Updates students can follow

Share periodic summaries of issues raised, referrals made, responses received and matters still pending—without exposing private details.

08
Accountability

Honest progress, not empty claims

Be clear about what has been done, what needs university approval and what still requires work.

02 / The first 100 days

Listen. Organise.
Report back.

A practical proposed roadmap for the first 100 days if elected. These are priorities to pursue through student consultation and the appropriate university channels—not guaranteed outcomes or claims of authority over university decisions.

01
Days 1–15 · Listen

Open the student listening desk

Set up clear channels for students to raise academic, hostel, facilities and welfare concerns; publish how submissions will be handled and who can access them.

02
Days 1–30 · Map

Build a campus issues register

Collect and group recurring concerns from departments and halls, remove duplicate reports, and record which office or authority is appropriate for each issue.

03
Days 15–40 · Meet

Department and hall consultations

Hold or coordinate listening meetings where feasible, with published dates only after confirmation and accessible ways for students to contribute.

04
Days 25–50 · Prioritise

Publish the top student concerns

Share an anonymised summary of the most repeated issues and proposed next steps, without exposing names, enrolment numbers or sensitive complaint details.

05
Days 35–70 · Refer

Submit documented representations

Forward prioritised, evidence-based concerns to the relevant university offices and seek acknowledgements or meetings where the process allows.

06
Days 50–80 · Support

Make student resources easier to find

Compile reliable official links for academic notices, scholarships, career resources and grievance channels, and flag information gaps to the relevant offices.

07
Days 70–90 · Review

Check status and follow up

Track issues as submitted, acknowledged, under review, resolved or awaiting response—using only statuses that can be verified.

08
Days 90–100 · Report

Publish a 100-day progress report

Report what was heard, what was formally raised, what changed, what remains pending and the next steps. Protect confidential information throughout.

Our standard: visible work, honest reporting. The 100-day roadmap will be updated with confirmed actions and evidence. University decisions, approvals and timelines remain with the relevant authorities.

04 / Student concerns · AMU

CPC students’
concerns deserve answers.

Questions raised by CPC students about AMUSU election voting eligibility and university hall allotment. The campaign calls for transparent rules and a clear response from the administration.

CPC STUDENTS’ CONCERNS | AMU

Fair hearing. Transparent rules. Clear answers.

CPC students have raised concerns regarding the absence of voting rights in AMUSU elections despite being internal students, along with the non-allotment of university halls.

Our stand: Every student’s concerns deserve a fair hearing, transparent rules, and a clear response from the administration.

Key questions

  • Why are CPC students not eligible to vote?
  • Why are halls not allotted to them?
  • What do the official rules say, and what solution can be provided?

The questions are presented as concerns requiring clarification. Confirm the applicable official rules and the administration’s response before publishing claims about eligibility or policy.

AMU Halls of Residence — Student Concerns and Representation

Every student deserves a safe, hygienic and well-maintained residential environment. This platform brings together hall-wise concerns raised by students, from dining and room allotment to sanitation, infrastructure, connectivity, security and student safety.

Our objective: Promote transparent student representation, responsible administration and practical solutions through systematic documentation.

Our commitment: Every concern deserves a fair hearing. Every complaint deserves appropriate attention. Every student deserves dignity, safety and a better living environment.

These are concerns supplied for the campaign and have not been independently verified. Publish updates about actions taken only when confirmed. Avoid including identifying or sensitive personal details.
